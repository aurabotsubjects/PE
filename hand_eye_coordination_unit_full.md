# Hand–Eye Coordination Unit — Full 20-Lesson Build

### Companion file to the Years 5/6 Game-Based Physical Education Program

---

This is the full 20-lesson build for the **Hand–Eye Coordination** cross-cutting focus unit, following the ERO-ready lesson plan template set out in Section 10 of the main program plan (`pe_program_plan.md`), using the same structure as the Soccer, Rippa Rugby, Netball, Basketball, Hockey, Volleyball, Cricket, Teeball, Tennis, Team Play, and Strategy & Decision-Making unit builds.

**Format:** A sport-neutral small-sided games format — bibs for team identification, a range of balls (soft/foam balls, tennis-style reaction balls, and whatever the school has on hand), soft bats/paddles, and cones — deliberately kept adaptable so the *tracking, catching, striking, reacting, and timing* skills, not any one sport's technique, stay the focus throughout.

**Class size:** 20–30 students · **Space:** Any large open space — field, hall, or court (fully adaptable) · **Session length:** 45 minutes, fixed · **Structure:** 4 blocks × 5 lessons

## Block Overview

| Block | Lessons | Focus | Warm-Up | Warm-Down |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–5 | Tracking & Catching | Track & Toss Jog | Walk & Reach |
| 2 | 6–10 | Striking & Contact | Strike Prep Shuffle | Partner Stretch |
| 3 | 11–15 | Reaction & Timing | Quick Reaction Jog | Breathe & Balance |
| 4 | 16–20 | Combining Skills & Culmination | Coordination Circuit Jog | Circle Stretch & Cheer |

## Lesson 1 of 20 — "Eyes On It"

**Block 1 — Tracking & Catching** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Tracking an object with your eyes all the way into your hands

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to watch a ball all the way into our hands, not look away early.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... keep my eyes on the ball right until it touches my hands
- I can... catch a gently thrown ball cleanly using both hands

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Movement skills

**School Values Focus:** Aroha — Being patient and encouraging with teammates whose skills or confidence look different from yours, and treating everyone with kindness whether the game is going well or not.

**Equipment:** 1 soft/foam ball per pair, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Soft balls only for this early tracking work; standard spacing between pairs

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Watch the ball the whole way, right into your hands, not just until it's close.** *Why:* Looking away early is the single biggest reason a catch is dropped — the eyes need to finish the job the hands are about to do.
2. **Get your hands ready and in front of your body before the ball arrives.** *Why:* Hands prepared early can adjust smoothly instead of stabbing late at a moving target.
3. **Relax your hands rather than tensing up.** *Why:* Stiff hands bounce a ball away; relaxed hands absorb it.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Track & Toss Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Watch It In" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, one gently underarm throws a soft ball while the partner calls out the colour/pattern on the ball just before it lands in their hands.
- **Coaching point:** Say it as it lands, not before.
- **Easier variant:** Bigger, brighter ball, thrown from closer.
- **Harder variant:** Smaller ball, thrown from further away.
- **Chaos lever:** Random fake throws (partner pretends to throw)

**Skill Game 2 — "Eyes Stay On" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Standing in a circle, students pass a ball around while the teacher checks that eyes stay on the ball right up until it's caught, not on the thrower's face.
- **Coaching point:** Follow the ball's whole flight path.
- **Easier variant:** Slower, higher passes.
- **Harder variant:** Faster, flatter passes.
- **Chaos lever:** Teacher randomly calls 'switch direction'

**Skill Game 3 — "Catch & Freeze" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In small groups, players throw and catch on the move, freezing in a strong catching position the instant they've secured the ball.
- **Coaching point:** Freeze the moment it's in your hands.
- **Easier variant:** Walking pace only.
- **Harder variant:** Light jogging pace.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Big Game — "Track & Catch Tag" (12 min)**
- 4v4 keep-away style game where a clean tracked catch (eyes visibly on the ball throughout) earns a bonus point on top of standard possession scoring.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What happened when you looked away from the ball too early?
- How did getting your hands ready early change your catch?
- What did a 'freeze' catch feel like compared to a rushed one?
- *(Values link — Aroha):* How did Aroha show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Keeps eyes on the ball right into the hands
- Prepares hands early, in front of the body
- Catches with relaxed, absorbing hands

**Inclusion Notes:** Use bigger, brighter, softer balls and slower throws for students building tracking confidence; allow two hands for every catch early on.

## Lesson 2 of 20 — "Soft Hands"

**Block 1 — Tracking & Catching** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Cushioning/absorbing the ball on catching rather than catching rigid

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to catch with soft hands, giving a little as the ball arrives so it doesn't bounce away.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... let my hands 'give' slightly as I catch
- I can... catch without the ball bouncing out of my hands

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Movement skills

**School Values Focus:** Mōhio — Understanding your own strengths and how you fit into the team, and noticing what your teammates need from you in order to do their best.

**Equipment:** 1 ball per pair (variety of ball sizes), cones

**Safety Considerations:** Keep throws controlled and at a catchable height to avoid jammed fingers

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Let your hands move slightly backward as the ball arrives, absorbing its speed.** *Why:* Giving with the catch spreads out the force so the ball settles instead of bouncing off stiff hands.
2. **Cup your hands rather than holding them flat.** *Why:* A cupped shape matches the ball better and stops it slipping straight through.
3. **Bring the ball in toward your body once it's caught.** *Why:* Securing it against your body locks the catch in, especially under any pressure.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Track & Toss Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Give and Catch" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, one throws softly while the other practises pulling their hands back slightly on contact, exaggerating the 'give'.
- **Coaching point:** Let it sink in, don't stop it dead.
- **Easier variant:** Very soft, slow throws.
- **Harder variant:** Firmer throws.
- **Chaos lever:** Partner varies throw speed without warning

**Skill Game 2 — "Cup It" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Students catch a range of different sized balls (small, medium, large) focusing on shaping their hands into a cup before each catch.
- **Coaching point:** Shape your hands before it arrives, not after.
- **Easier variant:** Same ball size each turn.
- **Harder variant:** Ball size changes every throw.
- **Chaos lever:** Mystery ball swap mid-round

**Skill Game 3 — "Bounce-Out Battle" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In small groups, players count how many catches in a row they can make without the ball bouncing out of their hands, resetting the count if it does.
- **Coaching point:** Soft hands beat a strong grab.
- **Easier variant:** Slower pace, more time between catches.
- **Harder variant:** Quicker succession of catches.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Big Game — "Soft Hands Showdown" (12 min)**
- 4v2 keep-away where every clean, secure catch (no bounce-out) scores a bonus point, on top of possession scoring.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did 'giving' with the catch actually feel like?
- Did cupping your hands change how secure the catch felt?
- What happened when you tried to catch with stiff or flat hands?
- *(Values link — Mōhio):* How did Mōhio show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Hands give slightly on contact
- Hands cupped before the ball arrives
- Ball is secured against the body after catching

**Inclusion Notes:** Use larger, softer balls for students still building grip confidence; allow extra attempts without penalty while the 'give' technique develops.

## Lesson 3 of 20 — "Two Hands or One?"

**Block 1 — Tracking & Catching** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Choosing appropriate catching technique (two-handed vs one-handed) for the situation

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning when to use two hands and when one hand is needed to catch successfully.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... use two hands when I have time to get in position
- I can... reach and catch with one hand when the ball is out of two-hand range

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Movement skills

**School Values Focus:** Manaaki — Looking after your teammates' success as much as your own — including, encouraging, and setting others up to succeed.

**Equipment:** Balls, cones, bibs

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing; controlled throw heights when practising one-handed reaching catches

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Use two hands whenever you have time to get into position.** *Why:* Two hands give the most secure, reliable catch, so they're the default whenever possible.
2. **Reach with one hand only when the ball is genuinely out of two-hand range.** *Why:* A one-handed reach should be a last resort for distance, not a shortcut when two hands were available.
3. **Recover quickly into a balanced position after a one-handed catch.** *Why:* A one-handed catch is only useful if you can do something with the ball straight afterwards.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Track & Toss Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Two Hands First" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, receivers must get their whole body in line and use two hands whenever the throw is within easy reach.
- **Coaching point:** Move your feet, not just your hands.
- **Easier variant:** Throws always within easy reach.
- **Harder variant:** Occasional throws just out of easy reach to test judgement.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 2 — "Just Out of Reach" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Partner throws balls slightly wide so the receiver must judge whether to stretch with one hand or step to use two.
- **Coaching point:** Choose the option that actually gets the ball.
- **Easier variant:** Throws only slightly wide.
- **Harder variant:** Throws further out, requiring a genuine reach decision.
- **Chaos lever:** Mixed easy and hard throws in the same set

**Skill Game 3 — "Call It" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Before each throw, the receiver calls 'two' or 'one' based on where they think the ball will arrive, then attempts the catch.
- **Coaching point:** Trust your first read.
- **Easier variant:** Thrower signals direction early.
- **Harder variant:** No warning given.
- **Chaos lever:** Thrower occasionally fakes direction

**Big Game — "Catch Call" (12 min)**
- Small-sided keep-away game where players earn a bonus point for correctly judging and executing either a two-handed or one-handed catch based on the ball's position.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- How did you decide between one hand and two?
- When did a one-handed catch actually save the play?
- What happens if you always try to catch with just one hand?
- *(Values link — Manaaki):* How did Manaaki show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Uses two hands by default when there's time
- Reaches with one hand only when genuinely needed
- Recovers into a balanced position after catching

**Inclusion Notes:** Allow two-handed catches for every attempt for students still building confidence; introduce one-handed reaching only once two-handed catching is secure.

## Lesson 4 of 20 — "High and Low"

**Block 1 — Tracking & Catching** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Adjusting hands and body position to different flight heights (high balls, low balls, bouncing balls)

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to adjust our hands and body to catch balls that arrive high, low, or bouncing.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... adjust my hand position for a high, low, or bouncing ball
- I can... get my body behind the ball whenever I can

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Movement skills

**School Values Focus:** Pānga — Seeing how your role today connects to the bigger team effort — a small contribution that matters once it's put together with everyone else's.

**Equipment:** Balls, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing; keep bouncing-ball throws controlled so they don't bounce into other groups

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Turn your hands to match the height of the ball — fingers up for high balls, fingers down for low ones.** *Why:* Hand shape needs to match the ball's approach angle, or it simply won't be secure.
2. **Get your body behind the ball's line whenever possible.** *Why:* A body positioned behind the ball gives a second chance to control it even if the hands don't get it clean.
3. **Watch a bouncing ball all the way through the bounce, not just before it.** *Why:* A bounce changes the ball's path, so tracking has to continue through it, not stop beforehand.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Track & Toss Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "High or Low" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Partner throws a mix of high, chest-height, and low balls, and the receiver adjusts hand shape each time.
- **Coaching point:** Match your hands to where it's arriving.
- **Easier variant:** Clearly high or clearly low throws only.
- **Harder variant:** Throws close to shoulder or waist height, harder to judge.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 2 — "Bounce Read" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, one bounces the ball to the other, who must track it all the way through the bounce before catching.
- **Coaching point:** Watch it hit the ground, not just before.
- **Easier variant:** One controlled bounce, close range.
- **Harder variant:** Varying bounce height and angle.
- **Chaos lever:** Occasional double-bounce thrown in

**Skill Game 3 — "Body Behind It" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Small group game where receivers must move their feet to get their whole body behind the ball's line before catching, rather than reaching sideways.
- **Coaching point:** Feet first, then hands.
- **Easier variant:** Slower-moving balls, more time to adjust.
- **Harder variant:** Quicker, more varied throws.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation every minute

**Big Game — "All Heights" (12 min)**
- 4v4 small-sided game where a mix of high, low, and bounced passes are all in play, with a bonus point for a clean, well-adjusted catch at any height.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- How did your hands change shape for a high ball compared to a low one?
- What did tracking a bounce all the way through actually look like?
- Why does getting your body behind the ball help, even if your hands don't catch it cleanly?
- *(Values link — Pānga):* How did Pānga show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Adjusts hand shape to match ball height
- Moves feet to get body behind the ball
- Tracks a bouncing ball all the way through the bounce

**Inclusion Notes:** Start with clearly high or clearly low throws before mixing heights; allow a short pause before each throw for students still building this judgement.

## Lesson 5 of 20 — "Catch It All"

**Block 1 — Tracking & Catching** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Combining tracking, soft hands, catch-type choice, and height adjustment (Block 1 wrap-up)

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to bring together everything from this week — tracking, soft hands, and adjusting to height — into confident catching.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... use tracking, soft hands, and the right catch type together
- I can... catch confidently across a range of heights and speeds

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Movement skills

**School Values Focus:** Aroha — Being patient and encouraging with teammates whose skills or confidence look different from yours, and treating everyone with kindness whether the game is going well or not.

**Equipment:** Balls (various sizes), bibs, cones — combined equipment from Block 1 games for the wrap-up carousel

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing; combined activities revisited at a controlled pace

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Trust the tracking, soft hands, and height-adjustment skills you've built this week, all at once.** *Why:* A confident catch isn't one skill — it's several skills happening together without having to think about each one separately.
2. **Choose the right catch type instantly, without pausing to decide.** *Why:* By this point the decision between one or two hands should be fast and automatic, not a hesitation.
3. **Stay relaxed and ready between catches, not just during them.** *Why:* Good catchers are ready before the ball arrives, not scrambling once it does.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Track & Toss Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Coordination Carousel" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Teams rotate through short stations that each revisit a Block 1 focus — tracking, soft hands, catch-type choice, height adjustment.
- **Coaching point:** Use everything you've practised this week.
- **Easier variant:** Longer time per station.
- **Harder variant:** Shorter time, quicker rotation.
- **Chaos lever:** Head-to-head mirror

**Skill Game 2 — "Mixed Bag Catching" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, throws vary randomly in height, speed, and distance so the receiver must combine all of this week's skills on every catch.
- **Coaching point:** Read it fresh every time.
- **Easier variant:** Predictable pattern of throws.
- **Harder variant:** Fully randomised throws.
- **Chaos lever:** Caller occasionally shouts a trick instruction ('clap first!')

**Skill Game 3 — "Streak Catch" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Small groups count their longest run of clean, confident catches in a row, mixing heights and speeds.
- **Coaching point:** Stay relaxed, don't rush.
- **Easier variant:** Slower pace, generous spacing.
- **Harder variant:** Quicker pace, tighter spacing.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Big Game — "Catch It All Games" (12 min)**
- Small-sided matches where teams earn a bonus point for clean, confident catches across a range of heights and speeds, wrapping up the whole block.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- Which of this week's catching skills do you feel most confident with now?
- What did combining all of them together feel like today?
- What's still tricky when the ball comes in fast or awkward?
- *(Values link — Aroha):* How did Aroha show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Combines tracking, soft hands, and correct catch type together
- Adjusts confidently across a range of heights
- Stays relaxed and ready between catches

**Inclusion Notes:** Allow students to nominate their own 'easier' or 'harder' station variant based on confidence; celebrate streaks of any length to build confidence going into Block 2.

## Lesson 6 of 20 — "Meet the Ball"

**Block 2 — Striking & Contact** (Lessons 6–10)

**Focus Skill:** Watching the ball onto the striking surface (bat, hand, paddle) before making contact

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to watch the ball all the way onto our striking surface before we hit it.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... keep my eyes on the ball right until contact
- I can... make clean contact more often than I miss

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Movement skills

**School Values Focus:** Mōhio — Understanding your own strengths and how you fit into the team, and noticing what your teammates need from you in order to do their best.

**Equipment:** Soft bats/paddles or hands, soft balls, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Spacing between pairs so swings don't connect with a neighbouring group; soft balls only

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Watch the ball all the way onto your striking surface, not just until you start swinging.** *Why:* Most mishits happen because the eyes leave the ball a fraction too early, right when accuracy matters most.
2. **Get your striking surface (hand, bat, paddle) moving early so it's ready in time.** *Why:* A late-starting swing forces a rushed, inaccurate contact.
3. **Aim for the middle of the ball, not the edges.** *Why:* Centre contact is what actually sends the ball where you intend.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Strike Prep Shuffle (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Watch It Meet" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, one gently feeds a soft ball for the other to strike with an open hand or soft paddle, calling out the ball's colour just before contact.
- **Coaching point:** Say it right as you hit it.
- **Easier variant:** Slower, closer feeds.
- **Harder variant:** Faster, further feeds.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 2 — "Early Swing" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Students practise starting their swing or hand movement early, so it's already in motion before the ball arrives.
- **Coaching point:** Start moving before it gets close.
- **Easier variant:** Feeder gives a clear countdown.
- **Harder variant:** No countdown given.
- **Chaos lever:** Feeder varies timing of the feed

**Skill Game 3 — "Middle of the Ball" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In small groups, students aim to strike the centre of a stationary or gently rolling ball, with feedback on where contact was made.
- **Coaching point:** Picture the middle before you swing.
- **Easier variant:** Stationary ball.
- **Harder variant:** Slow rolling ball.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Big Game — "Clean Contact Challenge" (12 min)**
- Small-sided striking game (e.g. simple bat-and-ball or hand-strike format) where clean, centred contact earns a bonus point on top of standard scoring.

**Warm-Down — Partner Stretch (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What happened when your eyes left the ball too early?
- Did starting your swing earlier change how clean your contact was?
- What did centre contact actually feel like compared to an edge hit?
- *(Values link — Mōhio):* How did Mōhio show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Eyes stay on the ball right until contact
- Striking surface begins moving early
- Contact is made near the centre of the ball

**Inclusion Notes:** Use larger, softer, slower-moving balls for students building striking confidence; allow a stationary ball to strike from before introducing movement.

## Lesson 7 of 20 — "Sweet Spot"

**Block 2 — Striking & Contact** (Lessons 6–10)

**Focus Skill:** Finding the centre/sweet spot of the striking surface for cleaner, more controlled contact

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to find the sweet spot on our striking surface so our hits are cleaner and more controlled.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... make contact near the middle of my bat or hand
- I can... notice the difference between a sweet-spot hit and an edge hit

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Movement skills

**School Values Focus:** Manaaki — Looking after your teammates' success as much as your own — including, encouraging, and setting others up to succeed.

**Equipment:** Soft bats/paddles, soft balls, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing between striking pairs; soft equipment only

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Know roughly where the sweet spot is on your bat, paddle, or hand.** *Why:* You can't aim for something you haven't identified — knowing the target is the first step.
2. **Adjust your body position so the ball arrives at that sweet spot, not wherever it happens to land.** *Why:* Moving to the ball is more reliable than hoping the ball comes to a fixed swing.
3. **Notice and describe the difference in feel and sound between a clean and a mishit strike.** *Why:* Recognising the feedback helps you self-correct without needing to be told every time.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Strike Prep Shuffle (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Find the Spot" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Students experiment striking a slow, gently fed ball at different points on their bat/paddle/hand, noticing where it feels and sounds cleanest.
- **Coaching point:** Notice the difference, don't just swing.
- **Easier variant:** Very slow, close feeds.
- **Harder variant:** Slightly faster feeds.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 2 — "Move to the Spot" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, the feeder varies where the ball arrives slightly, and the striker must move their feet so the sweet spot meets the ball.
- **Coaching point:** Move your feet to the ball, not your bat to a fixed spot.
- **Easier variant:** Small variations in feed position.
- **Harder variant:** Larger variations.
- **Chaos lever:** Feeder calls 'freeze' — striker must adjust instantly

**Skill Game 3 — "Sound Check" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Small groups strike in turn and call out 'clean' or 'edge' based on the sound and feel, checked against what the feeder saw.
- **Coaching point:** Trust what you felt and heard.
- **Easier variant:** Feeder confirms after every hit.
- **Harder variant:** Feeder only confirms occasionally.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Big Game — "Sweet Spot Scoring" (12 min)**
- Small-sided striking game where clean, sweet-spot contact (spotted by the teacher or partner) earns bonus points on top of standard scoring.

**Warm-Down — Partner Stretch (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did the sweet spot actually feel or sound like compared to an edge hit?
- How did moving your feet help you find the sweet spot more often?
- What's one thing you'll try to fix about your contact next time?
- *(Values link — Manaaki):* How did Manaaki show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Identifies roughly where their sweet spot is
- Moves feet to bring the sweet spot to the ball
- Notices and describes clean versus mishit contact

**Inclusion Notes:** Use bigger striking surfaces (wider bats/paddles) for students still building contact consistency; allow extra feeds without penalty while this feel develops.

## Lesson 8 of 20 — "Set Up Early"

**Block 2 — Striking & Contact** (Lessons 6–10)

**Focus Skill:** Getting body and hands/bat into position before contact (preparation and stance)

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to set up our body and bat or hands early, before the ball arrives, not at the last second.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... get into a ready position before the ball is close
- I can... adjust my set-up if the ball's path changes

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Movement skills

**School Values Focus:** Pānga — Seeing how your role today connects to the bigger team effort — a small contribution that matters once it's put together with everyone else's.

**Equipment:** Soft bats/paddles, soft balls, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing; controlled feed speeds while set-up habits are being built

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Get into a ready stance the moment you know the ball is coming, not once it's already close.** *Why:* A rushed, last-second set-up rarely produces clean contact — early preparation gives your body time to adjust.
2. **Keep adjusting your set-up as the ball's path becomes clearer.** *Why:* The first read isn't always perfect, so small adjustments as the ball approaches keep you lined up.
3. **Stay balanced and light on your feet in your ready position.** *Why:* A balanced stance lets you move quickly in any direction once you know exactly where the ball is going.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Strike Prep Shuffle (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Ready Early" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, the striker must be in a set, ready stance before the feeder releases the ball, checked by the feeder.
- **Coaching point:** Be ready before I even let go.
- **Easier variant:** Feeder gives a clear verbal countdown.
- **Harder variant:** No countdown, feeder chooses their own moment.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 2 — "Adjust and Strike" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Feeder varies the ball's path slightly after release, and the striker must make a small last-moment adjustment to their set-up before striking.
- **Coaching point:** Small adjustment, not a big scramble.
- **Easier variant:** Small, gentle path changes.
- **Harder variant:** Larger, later path changes.
- **Chaos lever:** Feeder occasionally fakes a path change

**Skill Game 3 — "Balanced and Ready" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Small groups practise moving between drills while returning to a balanced, ready stance each time before the next feed.
- **Coaching point:** Light feet, balanced base.
- **Easier variant:** More time between feeds.
- **Harder variant:** Quicker succession of feeds.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation every minute

**Big Game — "Set-Up Success" (12 min)**
- Small-sided striking game where being visibly set up and ready before each ball arrives earns a bonus point, on top of standard scoring for clean contact.

**Warm-Down — Partner Stretch (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**

**Safety Considerations:** Clear station boundaries and rotation system; standard spacing at each station

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Recognise which hand-eye skill each station needs, and apply it.** *Why:* Different challenges need different specific skills — a strong coordinator can tell the difference and adjust.
2. **Move between stations calmly and get ready quickly for the next challenge.** *Why:* A circuit works best when transitions are smooth, not rushed or chaotic.
3. **Use feedback from one station to improve at the next.** *Why:* These are all connected skills — a fix noticed in one spot often carries straight over to the next.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Coordination Circuit Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Station Rotation Warm-Up" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A quick rotation through three short stations (catching, striking, reaction) to warm into today's circuit format.
- **Coaching point:** Get the feel of each station before we go full circuit.
- **Easier variant:** Longer time per station.
- **Harder variant:** Shorter time, quicker rotation.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 2 — "Full Circuit Round 1" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Students complete a full rotation through all circuit stations at a steady, controlled pace.
- **Coaching point:** Bring your best technique to every station.
- **Easier variant:** More time per station.
- **Harder variant:** Less time per station.
- **Chaos lever:** Head-to-head mirror between two groups on the same station

**Skill Game 3 — "Full Circuit Round 2" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A second full rotation, with students aiming to beat their own personal result or streak from Round 1 at each station.
- **Coaching point:** Try to beat your own number, not someone else's.
- **Easier variant:** Same difficulty as Round 1.
- **Harder variant:** Slightly increased difficulty at each station.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus objectives added to one station

**Big Game — "Circuit Challenge Game" (12 min)**
- Teams combine their circuit results into a team score, then play a short small-sided game applying whichever circuit skills come up naturally in play.

**Warm-Down — Circle Stretch & Cheer (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- Which station felt strongest for you today, and which felt trickiest?
- What did you carry over from one station to the next?
- How did rotating between different challenges compare to practising just one thing?
- *(Values link — Aroha):* How did Aroha show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Applies the correct hand-eye skill at each different station
- Transitions between stations calmly and quickly
- Uses feedback from one station to improve at the next

**Inclusion Notes:** Allow flexible station order so students can build confidence at an easier station first; offer a buddy system for stations that involve reaction speed.

## Lesson 18 of 20 — "Game Ready"

**Block 4 — Combining Skills & Culmination** (Lessons 16–20)

**Focus Skill:** Applying hand-eye coordination skills inside a live, game-like context

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to use our catching, striking, and reaction skills inside a real, flowing game, not just in isolated drills.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... apply hand-eye skills accurately while a game is flowing around me
- I can... stay coordinated even as the game gets faster or more chaotic

**NZC Links:** Strand B, C

**School Values Focus:** Mōhio — Understanding your own strengths and how you fit into the team, and noticing what your teammates need from you in order to do their best.

**Equipment:** Balls, bibs, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing across simultaneous games; agreed, fair self-officiating routines

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Bring catching, striking, timing, and reaction skills into a live game setting, not just controlled drills.** *Why:* The real test of hand-eye coordination is whether it holds up once a game adds unpredictability and speed.
2. **Stay coordinated even as the pace and chaos of the game increase.** *Why:* Games rarely happen at a nice, controlled drill pace — coordination has to hold up under real conditions.
3. **Communicate clearly with teammates to support each other's coordination (calling for the ball, warning of a defender).** *Why:* Good communication makes everyone's hand-eye actions easier and more accurate.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Coordination Circuit Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Warm Into the Game" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A shortened, simplified version of today's game format, letting players find their rhythm and reacquaint their hand-eye skills with live play.
- **Coaching point:** Use this to settle in, not to win.
- **Easier variant:** Longer warm-in time.
- **Harder variant:** Shorter warm-in, straight into full games.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 2 — "Call and Catch" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In small-sided games, players must call for the ball clearly before receiving it, reinforcing communication alongside coordination.
- **Coaching point:** Call early, then catch clean.
- **Easier variant:** Slower-paced games.
- **Harder variant:** Faster-paced games.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 3 — "Bonus Skill Game" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A small-sided game where a bonus point is awarded any time a player clearly demonstrates a specific hand-eye skill from earlier in the unit (a soft-hands catch, a clean strike, a well-timed reach).
- **Coaching point:** Look for chances to use your best skills, not just any skill.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher points out bonus moments.
- **Harder variant:** Players self-identify bonus moments.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus objectives rotate mid-game

**Big Game — "Full Flowing Game" (12 min)**
- A complete small-sided game where students apply the whole range of hand-eye skills from across the unit inside genuine, fast-flowing play.

**Warm-Down — Circle Stretch & Cheer (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What was different about using these skills in a real game compared to a drill?
- How did communicating with teammates help your coordination?
- What happened to your coordination as the game got faster?
- *(Values link — Mōhio):* How did Mōhio show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Applies hand-eye skills accurately within live, flowing play
- Stays coordinated as pace and chaos increase
- Communicates clearly to support teammates' coordination

**Inclusion Notes:** Offer a simplified rule set or slower-paced group for students still building confidence in live-game coordination; celebrate effort and accurate attempts, not just successful outcomes.

## Lesson 20 of 20 — "Hand-Eye Coordination Games Day"

**Block 4 — Combining Skills & Culmination** (Lessons 16–20)

**Focus Skill:** Culminating multi-game/challenge day celebrating hand-eye coordination across the whole unit
