# Strategy & Decision-Making Unit — Full 20-Lesson Build

### Companion file to the Years 5/6 Game-Based Physical Education Program

---

This is the full 20-lesson build for the **Strategy & Decision-Making** cross-cutting focus unit, following the ERO-ready lesson plan template set out in Section 10 of the main program plan (`pe_program_plan.md`), using the same structure as the Soccer, Rippa Rugby, Netball, Basketball, Hockey, Volleyball, Cricket, Teeball, Tennis, and Team Play units.

**Format:** A sport-neutral small-sided games format — bibs for team identification, a generic ball (soft/foam ball, or whatever ball the school has on hand), and cones — deliberately kept adaptable so the *decision-making and tactical thinking* skills, not any one sport's technique, stay the focus throughout.

**Class size:** 20–30 students · **Space:** Any large open space — field, hall, or court (fully adaptable) · **Session length:** 45 minutes, fixed · **Structure:** 4 blocks × 5 lessons

## Block Overview

| Block | Lessons | Focus | Warm-Up | Warm-Down |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–5 | Reading the Game & Making a Choice | Scan & Spot Jog | Walk & Reach |
| 2 | 6–10 | Weighing Risk & Reward | Partner Choice Jog | Partner Stretch |
| 3 | 11–15 | Adapting Tactics & Team Plans | Read & Shift Shuffle | Breathe & Balance |
| 4 | 16–20 | Game Plans, Pressure & Culmination | Team Shape Jog | Circle Stretch & Cheer |

## Lesson 1 of 20 — "Eyes Up"

**Block 1 — Reading the Game & Making a Choice** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Scanning before acting (looking up to check options before receiving or deciding)

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to look up and scan our surroundings before we get the ball, so we already know our options.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... look around to check my options before the ball arrives
- I can... name what I saw when asked (space, teammates, defenders)

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Thinking

**School Values Focus:** Aroha — Being patient and encouraging with teammates whose skills or confidence look different from yours, and treating everyone with kindness whether the game is going well or not.

**Equipment:** Bibs (2 colours), 1 ball per group of 4, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing so scanning students don't collide while looking around; keep early activities at walking pace

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Scan the space around you before the ball arrives, not after.** *Why:* Deciding starts with seeing — a player who scans early already knows their options when the ball turns up.
2. **Check for open teammates, space, and defenders, roughly in that order.** *Why:* A simple scanning order stops the situation feeling overwhelming and speeds up decisions.
3. **Keep scanning even when you don't have the ball.** *Why:* A good decision when the ball arrives depends on a picture already built beforehand.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Scan & Spot Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Head Up Circle" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Standing in a circle passing a ball, students must lift their head and look at two other players before receiving, with the teacher checking eyes stay up.
- **Coaching point:** Look up before it arrives, not after.
- **Easier variant:** Slower passes, bigger circle.
- **Harder variant:** Two balls going at once.
- **Chaos lever:** Random freeze — 'what did you see?'

**Skill Game 2 — "Scan & Point" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In a moving grid, ball carriers dribble or jog while teammates without the ball silently point at open space they've spotted; the carrier passes toward a pointed space.
- **Coaching point:** Trust what you scanned, not what you guess.
- **Easier variant:** Walking pace only.
- **Harder variant:** Add a passive defender.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation every minute

**Skill Game 3 — "What Did You See?" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In a small group possession game, the teacher randomly freezes play and asks a non-ball-carrier to say aloud what options they'd scanned.
- **Coaching point:** Name the specific thing — a space, or a person.
- **Easier variant:** Freeze more often, more time to answer.
- **Harder variant:** Freeze less often, faster recall needed.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Big Game — "Scan & Score" (12 min)**
- 4v2 keep-away where players earn a bonus point for passing to an option they'd clearly scanned before receiving (spotted by the teacher), on top of points for keeping possession.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did you actually see when you looked up today?
- Did scanning early change how fast you could decide?
- What happens to your decisions when you only look at the ball?
- *(Values link — Aroha):* How did Aroha show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Lifts head to scan before receiving the ball
- Can name what they scanned when asked
- Scans repeatedly, not just once

**Inclusion Notes:** Allow more freeze-checks for students still building this habit; pair with a supportive partner who models scanning out loud.

## Lesson 2 of 20 — "Spot the Gap"

**Block 1 — Reading the Game & Making a Choice** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Recognising space and open options as a decision cue

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to spot open space and gaps so we know when there's a good option available.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... point out a gap or open teammate before acting
- I can... choose the option that gives my team the most space

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Thinking

**School Values Focus:** Mōhio — Understanding your own strengths and how you fit into the team, and noticing what your teammates need from you in order to do their best.

**Equipment:** Bibs (2 colours), 1 ball per group of 4, cones marking a grid

**Safety Considerations:** Clear grid boundaries so scanning for gaps doesn't send students into walls, fences, or other groups' games

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Look for gaps in the defence, not just where the ball is.** *Why:* The best decision often depends on space that has nothing to do with where the ball currently sits.
2. **Compare more than one option before choosing.** *Why:* The first option seen isn't always the best one — briefly comparing leads to better choices.
3. **Act on the gap quickly once you spot it.** *Why:* Gaps close fast, so noticing one is only useful if you use it straight away.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Scan & Spot Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Gap Hunters" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Students jog through a grid of cones representing defenders, calling out 'gap!' as they move through the space between two cones.
- **Coaching point:** Eyes up, not down at your feet.
- **Easier variant:** Wider gaps.
- **Harder variant:** Narrower, moving gaps (a partner shifts the cones).
- **Chaos lever:** Gaps randomly shift on a whistle

**Skill Game 2 — "Best Option Pass" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In a 3v1 keep-away game, the passer briefly names two options aloud ('A or B?') before passing to whichever is more open.
- **Coaching point:** Say both options before you choose.
- **Easier variant:** Defender walks only.
- **Harder variant:** Two defenders.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus for spotting a gap the defender didn't expect

**Skill Game 3 — "Race to the Gap" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Small teams compete to be first to move into a marked open gap when the teacher calls it out.
- **Coaching point:** Move the instant you see it, don't wait.
- **Easier variant:** Gaps stay marked and visible longer.
- **Harder variant:** Gaps appear and disappear quickly.
- **Chaos lever:** Fake gap calls to test focus

**Big Game — "Gap Attack" (12 min)**
- 4v4 small-sided game where teams earn a bonus point for a pass or run into a clearly identified gap in the defence, on top of standard scoring.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What makes a gap 'good' to use, not just open?
- How did comparing two options change your decision?
- What happened when you waited too long to use a gap?
- *(Values link — Mōhio):* How did Mōhio show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Identifies gaps or open options before acting
- Compares more than one option briefly
- Acts on a spotted gap without hesitating

**Inclusion Notes:** Use visual markers to highlight gaps for students who find spatial reading harder; allow verbal coaching cues ('look left') for those still building this skill.

## Lesson 3 of 20 — "Quick or Safe?"

**Block 1 — Reading the Game & Making a Choice** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Simple binary decision-making — choosing between a safe option and a riskier one

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to choose between a safe option and a quicker, riskier one, based on what the game actually needs.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... explain why I chose the safe or risky option in a moment
- I can... make a decision quickly instead of freezing

**NZC Links:** Strand B, C

**School Values Focus:** Manaaki — Looking after your teammates' success as much as your own — including, encouraging, and setting others up to succeed.

**Equipment:** Balls, bibs, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing; clear rules to prevent rushed or unsafe contact when moving quickly

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Notice when a situation calls for a safe option instead of a risky one.** *Why:* Not every moment needs a big risk — reading the moment first is what makes a decision a good one.
2. **Make your decision quickly rather than freezing between two options.** *Why:* Hesitating usually loses both options — quick, decisive choices keep opportunities alive.
3. **Be able to explain your choice afterwards.** *Why:* Naming the reason behind a decision is what turns it into a lesson for next time, not just a guess.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Scan & Spot Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Safe or Send It" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, the receiver calls 'safe' or 'go' as the ball approaches, and the passer must react instantly with a slower controlled pass or a faster driven one.
- **Coaching point:** React to the call, don't guess ahead.
- **Easier variant:** Caller gives more time before the pass.
- **Harder variant:** Caller changes their mind at the last second.
- **Chaos lever:** Mixed calls mid-rally

**Skill Game 2 — "Traffic Light Decisions" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In a small group game, the teacher or a peer holds up a coloured card (green = go for it, red = play safe) mid-play, and students adjust their next action instantly.
- **Coaching point:** Check the signal, then commit.
- **Easier variant:** Signal held up further in advance.
- **Harder variant:** Signal flashed briefly and randomly.
- **Chaos lever:** Double signal — one true, one decoy

**Skill Game 3 — "Why Did You Choose That?" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In short small-sided rounds, the teacher asks a player, right after a key moment, to explain in one sentence why they went safe or risky.
- **Coaching point:** One clear reason is enough.
- **Easier variant:** Multiple-choice reasons offered.
- **Harder variant:** No prompts, own words only.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Big Game — "Risk or Rest" (12 min)**
- Small-sided possession game where a flipped coloured cone mid-game signals whether the round rewards safe, controlled play or quick, risky play, and teams must adjust their decisions accordingly.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What told you a moment needed a safe choice instead of a risky one?
- Did explaining your choice out loud change how you saw it?
- When did hesitating cost your team an option today?
- *(Values link — Manaaki):* How did Manaaki show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Chooses between safe and risky options based on the situation
- Decides quickly rather than freezing
- Can explain the reasoning behind a decision

**Inclusion Notes:** Allow more thinking time in early rounds for students building decision confidence; accept a range of valid reasons rather than one 'correct' answer.

## Lesson 4 of 20 — "Read the Defender"

**Block 1 — Reading the Game & Making a Choice** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Reading an opponent's position and body language to inform a decision

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to read a defender's position and body language to decide our next move.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... notice which side a defender is favouring before I decide
- I can... choose a decision that uses what the defender is giving me

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Thinking

**School Values Focus:** Pānga — Seeing how your role today connects to the bigger team effort — a small contribution that matters once it's put together with everyone else's.

**Equipment:** Bibs, balls, cones

**Safety Considerations:** 1v1/2v1 activities kept to controlled speed, with clear space between groups

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Notice which way a defender is facing or leaning before deciding.** *Why:* A defender's position usually tells you which option they've already given up — reading it early makes the decision easier.
2. **Choose the option the defender is least protecting.** *Why:* Working with what's already open is more reliable than trying to force through where they're strongest.
3. **Recheck the defender's position if it changes.** *Why:* Defenders move and adjust, so a decision based on an old picture can quickly become the wrong one.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Scan & Spot Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Shadow Read" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, one student mirrors a slow-moving 'defender' while trying to read and call out which side is more open.
- **Coaching point:** Watch hips and feet, not just the ball.
- **Easier variant:** Defender moves slowly and obviously.
- **Harder variant:** Defender fakes direction.
- **Chaos lever:** Role swap on a whistle

**Skill Game 2 — "1v1 Gap Read" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A simple 1v1 grid game where the attacker chooses to go to the side the defender is showing them, scoring a bonus point for reading it correctly.
- **Coaching point:** Go where they're not.
- **Easier variant:** Defender must stay side-on the whole time.
- **Harder variant:** Defender can square up and reset.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus point for reading a fake

**Skill Game 3 — "2v1 Decision" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A classic 2v1 keep-away game where the ball carrier reads the single defender's position to decide whether to pass or carry themselves.
- **Coaching point:** Read the defender, not just your teammate.
- **Easier variant:** Defender must stay a set distance back.
- **Harder variant:** Defender can close down quickly.
- **Chaos lever:** Defender allowed one 'fake' movement per round

**Big Game — "Read & React" (12 min)**
- Small-sided game where teams earn a bonus point for attacking moves that visibly used a defender's position or body shape to make the decision, on top of standard scoring.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did a defender's position tell you today?
- How did reading the defender change your decision compared to guessing?
- What happened when the defender's position changed quickly?
- *(Values link — Pānga):* How did Pānga show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Notices a defender's position or body shape before deciding
- Chooses an option the defender is leaving open
- Rechecks and adjusts if the defender's position changes

**Inclusion Notes:** Start with slow, obvious defender movement for students building this reading skill; use simple verbal cues ('which way are they facing?') to prompt the read.

## Lesson 5 of 20 — "Decide and Commit"

**Block 1 — Reading the Game & Making a Choice** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Committing to a decision without hesitation (Block 1 wrap-up)

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to make a decision and commit to it fully, without hesitating or second-guessing halfway through.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... commit fully to a decision once I've made it
- I can... reflect on a decision afterwards without dwelling on a mistake

**NZC Links:** Strand B, C

**School Values Focus:** Aroha — Being patient and encouraging with teammates whose skills or confidence look different from yours, and treating everyone with kindness whether the game is going well or not.

**Equipment:** Balls, bibs, cones — combined equipment from Block 1 games for the wrap-up carousel

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing; remind students that hesitating mid-action can cause more collisions than committing fully

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Make your decision and commit to it fully, rather than changing your mind halfway.** *Why:* A committed action, even an imperfect one, usually works out better than a hesitant, half-changed one.
2. **Reflect briefly on a decision after it happens, then move on.** *Why:* A quick, honest reflection helps you learn without it turning into dwelling on a mistake.
3. **Trust the scanning, gap-reading, and defender-reading skills you've already practised this week.** *Why:* Good decisions come from trusting the picture you've already built, not from second-guessing it in the moment.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Scan & Spot Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Commit Challenge" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In short small-sided rounds, the teacher calls out 'commit!' the instant a player begins an action, rewarding full, committed execution over hesitant half-actions.
- **Coaching point:** Once you've started, follow all the way through.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher calls decisions early to build confidence.
- **Harder variant:** Decisions must be made and committed to instantly.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus for recovering well after a committed decision doesn't quite work

**Skill Game 2 — "Decision Carousel" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Teams rotate through short stations that each revisit a Block 1 focus — scanning, spotting gaps, safe vs risky choices, reading a defender.
- **Coaching point:** Use everything you've practised this week.
- **Easier variant:** Longer time per station.
- **Harder variant:** Shorter time, quicker rotation.
- **Chaos lever:** Head-to-head mirror

**Skill Game 3 — "No Regrets Round" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In small games, after every decision — whether it worked or not — the player briefly says what they saw and why they chose it, with no criticism allowed.
- **Coaching point:** State it, don't judge it.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher models the first few.
- **Harder variant:** Quick turnaround, no pause in play.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Big Game — "Commit & Reflect Games" (12 min)**
- Small-sided matches where teams earn a bonus point for committing fully to decisions and for giving each other quick, judgement-free feedback on choices, wrapping up the whole block.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did 'committing' to a decision actually feel like today?
- What's the difference between reflecting on a decision and dwelling on it?
- Looking back over this block, what's improved most in how your team makes decisions?
- *(Values link — Aroha):* How did Aroha show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Commits fully to a decision rather than hesitating or changing mid-action
- Reflects briefly and constructively on decisions
- Shows growth across Block 1 in scanning, reading, and choosing

**Inclusion Notes:** Model 'no regrets' reflection language for students who are hard on themselves after mistakes; allow extra thinking time in early rounds for those still building decision confidence.

## Lesson 6 of 20 — "Worth the Risk?"

**Block 2 — Weighing Risk & Reward** (Lessons 6–10)

**Focus Skill:** Weighing risk versus reward before attempting an ambitious option

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to weigh up whether a risky option is actually worth it before we try it.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... weigh up the risk and reward before attempting an ambitious play
- I can... choose the safer option when the risk clearly outweighs the reward

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Thinking

**School Values Focus:** Mōhio — Understanding your own strengths and how you fit into the team, and noticing what your teammates need from you in order to do their best.

**Equipment:** Balls, bibs, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing for small-sided games

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Ask what you actually gain if a risky option works.** *Why:* A risk is only worth it if the reward genuinely matters — decide that before you try it, not after.
2. **Ask what your team loses if it doesn't work.** *Why:* Knowing the cost of a failed risk is just as important as knowing the potential reward.
3. **Choose the safer option when the cost clearly outweighs the gain.** *Why:* Good decision-makers aren't afraid of risk, but they don't take one that costs more than it's worth.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Partner Choice Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Risk Points" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Small-sided possession game where a risky pass or move scores double points if it works but costs a turnover-style penalty if it doesn't, while safe options score a single guaranteed point.
- **Coaching point:** Is the double worth the risk right now?
- **Easier variant:** Smaller penalty for a failed risky option.
- **Harder variant:** Bigger reward and bigger cost, sharpening the decision.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus multiplier round announced at random

**Skill Game 2 — "Worth It? Card Call" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In a partner activity, before each attempt one partner quickly says 'worth it' or 'not worth it' and why, before the pair attempts the play.
- **Coaching point:** Say the reason, not just the verdict.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher offers example reasons.
- **Harder variant:** Quick-turnaround verdicts, no pause.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 3 — "Cost of the Fail" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In small games, every failed risky attempt is briefly discussed — what did it actually cost the team?
- **Coaching point:** Name the cost honestly, then move on.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher prompts the discussion point.
- **Harder variant:** Players self-identify the cost.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus point for the clearest cost explanation

**Big Game — "High Stakes Possession" (12 min)**
- Small-sided game with a genuine risk/reward scoring system — bigger reward for ambitious plays, real cost for failed ones — pushing teams to weigh decisions rather than always choosing one or the other.

**Warm-Down — Partner Stretch (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What made a risk 'worth it' today, and what made one not worth it?
- Did thinking about the cost change which option you chose?
- When did a safe choice turn out to be the smarter one?
- *(Values link — Mōhio):* How did Mōhio show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Weighs risk and reward before attempting an ambitious play
- Can explain what a risky option would cost if it failed
- Chooses the safer option when the risk clearly isn't worth it

**Inclusion Notes:** Offer simplified risk/reward language (e.g. 'big prize, big cost' vs 'small prize, safe') for younger or less confident decision-makers; allow extra discussion time before attempts early on.

## Lesson 7 of 20 — "Numbers Game"

**Block 2 — Weighing Risk & Reward** (Lessons 6–10)

**Focus Skill:** Reading numbers — attackers versus defenders — to decide when to go

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to count attackers versus defenders to decide when it's actually a good moment to attack.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... recognise when my team has more attackers than defenders in a space
- I can... decide to attack or hold based on the numbers, not just instinct

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Thinking

**School Values Focus:** Manaaki — Looking after your teammates' success as much as your own — including, encouraging, and setting others up to succeed.

**Equipment:** Bibs, balls, cones marking zones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing across overlapping small-sided zones

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Count how many attackers versus defenders are in the space before deciding to go.** *Why:* Numbers up — more attackers than defenders — is one of the clearest, most reliable signals that a moment is worth attacking.
2. **Hold or reset when the numbers aren't in your favour.** *Why:* Attacking into numbers down usually costs possession — waiting for a better moment is often the smarter decision.
3. **Call the numbers out loud to help teammates decide too.** *Why:* A quick call like 'numbers up!' helps the whole team recognise and use a good moment together, not just one player.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Partner Choice Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Count and Call" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Small groups move through a grid with defenders scattered through it; before every action, students must call out the number of attackers versus defenders nearby.
- **Coaching point:** Count before you decide, every time.
- **Easier variant:** Numbers stay obvious and fixed for longer.
- **Harder variant:** Numbers shift quickly as players move.
- **Chaos lever:** Random numbers-check freeze

**Skill Game 2 — "2v1, 3v2 Rotation" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Rotating small-sided overload games — 2v1, then 3v2 — where the attacking side must recognise and use the numbers advantage to attack directly.
- **Coaching point:** Numbers up means go, don't overthink it.
- **Easier variant:** Defender must stay further back.
- **Harder variant:** Numbers advantage reduced (e.g. 3v2 becomes tighter).
- **Chaos lever:** Sudden extra defender joins mid-play

**Skill Game 3 — "Numbers Up Call" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Possession game where a player who correctly calls 'numbers up!' in a genuinely favourable moment earns their team a bonus point.
- **Coaching point:** Call it the second you see it.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher confirms calls.
- **Harder variant:** Players must justify their own call.
- **Chaos lever:** False numbers announced by teacher to test genuine reading

**Big Game — "Overload Game" (12 min)**
- Small-sided game with regularly shifting overload situations (an extra attacker or defender rotated in), rewarding teams who recognise and attack genuine numbers-up moments rather than attacking blindly.

**Warm-Down — Partner Stretch (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did 'numbers up' actually look like today?
- How did counting change your decision compared to just going for it?
- What happened when your team attacked into numbers down?
- *(Values link — Manaaki):* How did Manaaki show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Recognises numbers-up situations before attacking
- Holds or resets when numbers aren't favourable
- Calls out numbers to help teammates decide

**Inclusion Notes:** Use simple visual cues (an extra bib colour for 'attacker') to make counting easier for students still building this skill; keep early rounds at a walking pace to allow time to count.

## Lesson 8 of 20 — "Plan B"

**Block 2 — Weighing Risk & Reward** (Lessons 6–10)

**Focus Skill:** Having a backup option ready if the first choice doesn't work

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to have a backup plan ready in case our first decision doesn't work out.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... think of a second option before I commit to my first
- I can... switch smoothly to my backup plan if my first choice is taken away

**NZC Links:** Strand B, C

**School Values Focus:** Pānga — Seeing how your role today connects to the bigger team effort — a small contribution that matters once it's put together with everyone else's.

**Equipment:** Balls, bibs, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing; clear communication when switching plans to avoid confusion or collisions

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Have a second option in mind before you commit to your first one.** *Why:* A defender can take away your first choice in an instant — having a backup ready means you're never caught with nothing.
2. **Switch to your backup smoothly, without panicking.** *Why:* A calm switch to Plan B looks and works far better than a rushed, panicked scramble.
3. **Communicate a plan change to your teammates quickly.** *Why:* A backup plan only works for the team if teammates know it's happening too.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Partner Choice Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Plan A, Plan B" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** In pairs, before each pass attempt the passer quietly names two options — Plan A and Plan B — to their partner, then switches to Plan B if a 'defender' cone blocks Plan A.
- **Coaching point:** Know your Plan B before you need it.
- **Easier variant:** Defender cone placement announced in advance.
- **Harder variant:** Defender cone appears at the last second.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation halfway

**Skill Game 2 — "'Blocked!' Reaction Game" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Small-sided game where the teacher or a defender can call or signal 'blocked!' on a player's intended option, forcing an instant, smooth switch to a backup.
- **Coaching point:** Switch calmly, don't freeze.
- **Easier variant:** Blocked calls given with more warning.
- **Harder variant:** Blocked calls given at the last possible moment.
- **Chaos lever:** Double-block forces a third option

**Skill Game 3 — "Call the Switch" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Possession game where players must verbally call out when they're switching from their first plan to a backup, so teammates can adjust too.
- **Coaching point:** Say it loud enough for your team to hear and react.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher prompts the call if forgotten.
- **Harder variant:** Fast-paced play, quick switches required.

## Lesson 18 of 20 — "Scout the Opposition"

**Block 4 — Game Plans, Pressure & Culmination** (Lessons 16–20)

**Focus Skill:** Reading and adapting to an unfamiliar opponent's style

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to read an unfamiliar opponent's style quickly and adapt our approach to it.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... identify something specific about an unfamiliar opponent's style early in a game
- I can... adjust my team's approach based on what we noticed

**NZC Links:** Strand B, C · Thinking

**School Values Focus:** Mōhio — Understanding your own strengths and how you fit into the team, and noticing what your teammates need from you in order to do their best.

**Equipment:** Bibs, balls, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing across simultaneous, rotating games

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Watch closely in the first few minutes against an unfamiliar opponent to identify something specific about their style.** *Why:* Every opponent has tendencies, and the first few minutes are often the best chance to spot them before they adjust to you.
2. **Share what you noticed with your team quickly.** *Why:* A quick, specific observation shared early can shape the whole team's approach for the rest of the game.
3. **Adjust your team's approach based on genuine observations, not assumptions.** *Why:* Reacting to what an opponent is actually doing, rather than what you expected, leads to better tactical decisions.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Team Shape Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "First Five Minutes" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Teams rotate through short games against unfamiliar opponents, focusing specifically on identifying one thing about the new opponent's style in the first few minutes.
- **Coaching point:** Watch closely before you judge.
- **Easier variant:** Longer observation window.
- **Harder variant:** Shorter observation window, quicker read required.
- **Chaos lever:** Opponent rotation speeds up

**Skill Game 2 — "Report Back" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Immediately after the observation window, each team quickly reports one specific thing they noticed about their opponent to the teacher or another team.
- **Coaching point:** Be specific, not general.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher offers example observation categories (speed, passing style, favourite space).
- **Harder variant:** Teams generate their own categories.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 3 — "Adjust and Play" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Teams use their scouting observation to make one specific adjustment to their approach for the rest of the game against that opponent.
- **Coaching point:** Match your adjustment to what you actually saw.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher supports the adjustment idea.
- **Harder variant:** Teams generate their own adjustment.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation

**Big Game — "Scout & Adapt Tournament" (12 min)**
- Round-robin small-sided games against a rotating set of unfamiliar opponents, where teams earn a bonus point for specific, accurate observations and effective tactical adjustments.

**Warm-Down — Circle Stretch & Cheer (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What specific thing did you notice about an unfamiliar opponent today?
- How did that observation change your team's approach?
- What's the risk of assuming things about an opponent instead of watching first?
- *(Values link — Mōhio):* How did Mōhio show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Identifies something specific about an unfamiliar opponent's style
- Shares observations clearly and quickly with the team
- Adjusts the team's approach based on genuine observation

**Inclusion Notes:** Offer simple observation categories (speed, passing, favourite space) to support students who find open-ended scouting harder; rotate opponents fairly so every team gets a genuine scouting challenge.
