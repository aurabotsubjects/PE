# Tennis Unit — Full 20-Lesson Build

### Companion file to the Years 5/6 Game-Based Physical Education Program

---

This is the full 20-lesson build for the **Tennis** sport unit, following the ERO-ready lesson plan template set out in Section 10 of the main program plan (`pe_program_plan.md`), using the same structure as the Soccer, Rippa Rugby, Netball, Basketball, Hockey, Volleyball, Cricket, and Teeball units.

**Format:** Tennis NZ's **Hot Shots** modified format — smaller court/net, low-compression ball — already familiar to most NZ schools/coaches (see Section 9 of the main plan).

**Class size:** 20–30 students · **Space:** Hard-court / marked Hot Shots courts (or hall as backup) · **Session length:** 45 minutes, fixed · **Structure:** 4 blocks × 5 lessons

## Block Overview

| Block | Lessons | Focus | Warm-Up | Warm-Down |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lessons 1–5 | Foundational Racquet & Ball Control | Bounce & Balance Relay | Walk & Reach |
| 2 | Lessons 6–10 | Combining Strokes, Footwork & Rallying | Partner Feed & Jog | Partner Stretch |
| 3 | Lessons 11–15 | Net Play, Reading the Ball & Decision-Making | Split-Step Shuffle | Breathe & Balance |
| 4 | Lessons 16–20 | Match Play & Culmination | Team Shape Jog | Circle Stretch & Cheer |

## Lesson 1 of 20 — "Shake Hands With Your Racquet"

**Block 1 — Foundational Racquet & Ball Control** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Racquet familiarisation & grip (forehand/backhand grip)

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to hold the racquet correctly for forehand and backhand, and to carry and control it safely while moving.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... show a correct forehand ‘shake hands’ grip and swap smoothly to a backhand grip
- I can... carry and control my racquet safely while moving around others

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Managing self

**School Values Focus:** Aroha — Being patient and kind with yourself while a new skill feels awkward, and encouraging classmates whose attempts look different from yours.

**Equipment:** 1 junior/Hot Shots racquet per student, cones for boundaries, spot markers

**Safety Considerations:** Racquets carried head-down/by the throat when walking near others; a clear ‘swing space’ (arm's length plus racquet length) from anyone else before swinging

### Skill Breakdown

1. **‘Shake hands’ with the racquet handle so the strings face sideways, like shaking someone's hand (forehand grip).** *Why:* This grip lets the racquet face meet the ball squarely without twisting the wrist.
2. **Rotate the hand slightly so the knuckles sit on top of the handle for backhand, or use two hands together for a two-handed backhand.** *Why:* A stable, consistent grip is the foundation every other stroke sits on.
3. **Hold the racquet with a relaxed hand — like holding a bird, not a hammer — gripping firmly only just before contact.** *Why:* A tense grip the whole time tires the arm and wobbles the racquet face; relaxed-then-firm gives control and power together.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Bounce & Balance Relay (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Racquet Freeze Parade" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Students jog around a marked area carrying their racquet correctly; on ‘freeze’ they show the grip the teacher calls (forehand or backhand) and hold a ready position.
- **Coaching point:** Check your grip by feel, not by looking down.
- **Easier variant:** Slower pace, only forehand grip called.
- **Harder variant:** Quick grip swaps called in sequence (forehand-backhand-forehand).
- **Chaos lever:** Random constraint calls

**Skill Game 2 — "Racquet Face Balance Walk" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Balance a ball on the racquet strings while walking a pathway of cones, using forehand- and backhand-side carries.
- **Coaching point:** Soft hands, slightly bent elbow, eyes on the ball.
- **Easier variant:** Stand still and balance first before adding walking.
- **Harder variant:** Add a change of direction or a light jog.
- **Chaos lever:** Beat the clock (count consecutive steps without dropping)

**Skill Game 3 — "Grip Swap Tag" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Partners jog together; on a signal, both swap grip (forehand to backhand or vice versa) as fast as possible and show it to their partner, who checks it's correct for a point.
- **Coaching point:** Feel the shape change in your hand, don't just twist the racquet.
- **Easier variant:** Partner calls the grip out loud in advance.
- **Harder variant:** No verbal cue — react to a visual card held up by partner.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation (checker/mover swap)

**Big Game — "Grip & Go Relay" (12 min)**
- Small teams relay: each runner sprints to a marked spot, shows the named grip correctly (checked by a teammate ‘coach’), balances the ball on the racquet back to the team, then tags the next runner. Combines today's two skills — grip and racquet control — under light time pressure and team encouragement.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did the ‘shake hands’ grip feel like once you got used to it?
- Who in your team showed a really clear, correct grip today — what did you notice?
- What was tricky about swapping grips quickly, and how did you work it out?
- *(Values link — Aroha):* How did Aroha show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Uses a recognisable ‘shake hands’ (forehand) grip without needing reminders
- Can swap to a backhand-side grip when asked
- Carries and controls the racquet safely around others

**Inclusion Notes:** Allow a two-handed grip for both forehand and backhand if that feels more stable early on; some students may need a lighter/shorter racquet — prioritise grip correctness over perfect technique on day one.

## Lesson 2 of 20 — "Bounce Boss"

**Block 1 — Foundational Racquet & Ball Control** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Bouncing & balancing the ball on the racquet (control)

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to control a ball by bouncing it up on the racquet face and bouncing it down onto the ground, using a soft, controlled touch.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... bounce the ball up on my racquet face several times in a row without losing control
- I can... bounce the ball down onto the ground and back up using my racquet

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Managing self

**School Values Focus:** Mōhio — Understanding your own body and how it moves — noticing exactly what's happening in your grip, swing, or footwork, and learning from it.

**Equipment:** 1 racquet + 1 low-compression ball per student, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Enough personal space so a rolling ball doesn't cause a collision; balls collected calmly rather than chased through others' spaces

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Keep the racquet face flat and steady, moving it slightly upward just as the ball touches it.** *Why:* A flat, steady face sends the ball straight back up; tilting it sends the ball flying off sideways.
2. **Use small, soft ‘give’ movements from the wrist and elbow rather than big swings.** *Why:* Small controlled movements are what let you bounce the ball many times in a row instead of smashing it away.
3. **Keep your eyes on the ball the whole time, not on your feet or racquet.** *Why:* In tennis you react to where the ball actually is — this habit starts here.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Bounce & Balance Relay (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Up-Bounce Count" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Individually bounce the ball up off the racquet face as many times in a row as possible; track a personal best.
- **Coaching point:** Small taps, flat racquet face.
- **Easier variant:** Bigger/softer ball; a ground bounce is allowed between racquet taps.
- **Harder variant:** Must also take three steps forward without stopping the bounce.
- **Chaos lever:** Beat the clock / personal-best streak

**Skill Game 2 — "Down-Bounce Dribble" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Like a basketball dribble but with the racquet: bounce the ball down onto the ground and back up while walking a pathway of cones.
- **Coaching point:** Push the ball, don't slap it.
- **Easier variant:** Stand still first before adding movement.
- **Harder variant:** Dribble while changing direction or speed on a call.
- **Chaos lever:** Random constraint calls

**Skill Game 3 — "Balance & Freeze" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Partners take turns bouncing the ball up on the racquet while walking; on ‘freeze’ they must balance the ball still on the racquet face without it rolling off, while their partner checks.
- **Coaching point:** Slightly cup the racquet face the instant you freeze.
- **Easier variant:** Fewer freeze calls, slower pace.
- **Harder variant:** Freeze while standing on one foot.
- **Chaos lever:** Movement penalty/reward

**Big Game — "Bounce Relay Challenge" (12 min)**
- Small teams relay a set distance, each runner keeping the ball under control with up-bounces before tagging the next teammate; team with the fewest drops (or fastest clean run) wins.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What helped you keep the ball under control today?
- Who found a good rhythm with their bouncing — what did their racquet look like?
- What did you do differently after you dropped the ball a few times?
- *(Values link — Mōhio):* How did Mōhio show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Uses a flat, steady racquet face rather than a tilted/slapping motion
- Strings together several controlled bounces in a row
- Eyes track the ball rather than the feet

**Inclusion Notes:** Let students who are struggling use a bigger, softer ball, or bounce off the ground between racquet touches; celebrate personal-best streaks rather than comparing students to each other.

## Lesson 3 of 20 — "Perfect Swing"

**Block 1 — Foundational Racquet & Ball Control** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Forehand groundstroke technique (off a feed/self-drop)

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning the technique of a forehand groundstroke — turning side-on, swinging low-to-high, and making contact out in front of the body.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... turn side-on and swing low-to-high to hit a self-dropped or fed ball
- I can... make contact with the ball out in front of my body, not beside or behind it

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Managing self

**School Values Focus:** Manaaki — Looking after a partner's success as much as your own — feeding, rallying, and encouraging in a way that helps them succeed too.

**Equipment:** 1 racquet + balls per pair, cones to mark a hitting line

**Safety Considerations:** Feeders and hitters spaced well apart from neighbouring pairs; check the swing zone is clear before any swing

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Turn side-on to the ball with your non-hitting shoulder pointing toward where you want the ball to go.** *Why:* A side-on turn lets you swing across your body with power and accuracy, instead of just poking at the ball facing forward.
2. **Swing the racquet from low to high, like scooping upward, brushing up the back of the ball.** *Why:* A low-to-high path naturally lifts the ball up and over the net with control instead of into the net or way over.
3. **Make contact with the ball out in front of your body, roughly level with your front hip.** *Why:* Hitting the ball out in front gives you the best combination of power and control — behind your body you lose both.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Bounce & Balance Relay (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Self-Drop Target" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Each student self-drops and forehands the ball toward a large marked zone; count clean hits into the zone.
- **Coaching point:** Turn side-on before you drop it.
- **Easier variant:** Bigger target zone, closer distance.
- **Harder variant:** Smaller target zone, further distance.
- **Chaos lever:** Scoring streaks

**Skill Game 2 — "Feeder & Hitter" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** One partner gently underarm feeds/bounces the ball, the other forehands it back toward a target; swap roles regularly.
- **Coaching point:** Good feeds get good hits — feed with care.
- **Easier variant:** Feeder stands very close with a slow, gentle feed.
- **Harder variant:** Feeder varies feed distance/height slightly.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation

**Skill Game 3 — "Shadow Swing Mirror" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Partners face each other and mirror a slow-motion forehand swing (no ball), checking each other's side-on turn and low-to-high path, then try it with a self-drop.
- **Coaching point:** Match your partner's turn before you match their swing.
- **Easier variant:** Call out each part of the swing step by step.
- **Harder variant:** Full-speed continuous shadow swings.
- **Chaos lever:** Head-to-head mirror

**Big Game — "Forehand Target Battle" (12 min)**
- Small groups take turns self-dropping and hitting forehands at a shared set of scoring zones (close zone = 1pt, far zone = 3pt); technique — side-on turn, low-to-high swing — is what makes the harder zones reachable.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did turning side-on do for your swing?
- Who fed the ball really well for their partner today — what made it a good feed?
- What's the difference between hitting the ball out in front versus beside you?
- *(Values link — Manaaki):* How did Manaaki show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Turns side-on before swinging
- Swings low-to-high with a visible upward brush on the ball
- Contacts the ball out in front of the body

**Inclusion Notes:** Allow closer self-drop/feed distances and bigger target zones for students building confidence; pair students so feeders are calm and patient, rotating pairs if a combination isn't working well.

## Lesson 4 of 20 — "Target Ace"

**Block 1 — Foundational Racquet & Ball Control** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Forehand accuracy to a target

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to aim a forehand groundstroke at a chosen target, using our follow-through and racquet face to control direction.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... aim my forehand toward a target on the left or right, not just anywhere
- I can... adjust my follow-through direction to help control where the ball goes

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Thinking

**School Values Focus:** Pānga — Seeing how today's skill connects to the bigger game of tennis — a small piece that matters once it's put together with everything else.

**Equipment:** racquets, balls, cones/hoops or marked zones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing between hitting pairs; check the target/swing zone is clear before hitting

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Choose a specific target — a hoop, cone gap, or zone — before you hit, not after.** *Why:* Deciding where to aim before you swing is what turns technique into accuracy.
2. **Point your follow-through (where your racquet finishes) toward your target.** *Why:* The path your racquet finishes on is a strong clue to where the ball actually went — a controlled follow-through means a controlled shot.
3. **Adjust the angle of your racquet face slightly left or right depending on which side of the target you're missing.** *Why:* Small face adjustments are what fine-tune direction once your basic swing is already working.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Bounce & Balance Relay (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Zone Target Forehand" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Self-drop forehands aimed at three marked zones (left, middle, right) worth different points; call the zone before hitting.
- **Coaching point:** Decide, then swing.
- **Easier variant:** Two wide zones.
- **Harder variant:** Three narrower zones, called by a partner rather than chosen by the hitter.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus objectives (harder zone = bonus points)

**Skill Game 2 — "Hoop Hunter" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A partner places or moves a hoop around a marked area; the hitter forehands from a self-drop to land the ball in or near the hoop, then roles swap.
- **Coaching point:** Aim before you drop the ball, not after.
- **Easier variant:** Hoop stationary and close.
- **Harder variant:** Hoop moved between hits, further away.
- **Chaos lever:** Random constraint calls (target moves)

**Skill Game 3 — "Streak Target Challenge" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Consecutive successful hits to the same called target before a miss resets the streak — a friendly challenge against your own best.
- **Coaching point:** After a miss, reset calmly and try the same target again.
- **Easier variant:** Bigger target.
- **Harder variant:** Alternate between left and right targets each hit.
- **Chaos lever:** Scoring streaks

**Big Game — "Forehand Precision Cup" (12 min)**
- Small groups rotate through a mini target-course of forehand zones worth escalating points; team totals build across rounds, rewarding precision, not just power.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What helped you actually hit the target you were aiming for?
- Who improved their accuracy during the lesson — what did they change?
- How does watching your follow-through help you understand your own shot?
- *(Values link — Pānga):* How did Pānga show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Selects a target before swinging
- Follow-through points toward the intended target
- Makes small adjustments after a miss rather than repeating the same error

**Inclusion Notes:** Widen target zones or shorten the distance for students still building basic forehand technique; let confident hitters challenge themselves with narrower zones or longer distances.

## Lesson 5 of 20 — "Flip Side"

**Block 1 — Foundational Racquet & Ball Control** (Lessons 1–5)

**Focus Skill:** Backhand groundstroke technique

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning the technique of a backhand groundstroke — turning side-on the opposite way to forehand, and swinging low-to-high.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... turn side-on (the opposite way to forehand) for a backhand
- I can... swing low-to-high to hit a self-dropped or fed ball on my backhand side

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Managing self

**School Values Focus:** Aroha — Being patient and kind with yourself while a new skill feels awkward, and encouraging classmates whose attempts look different from yours.

**Equipment:** racquets, balls, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Feeders and hitters spaced well apart from neighbouring pairs; check the swing zone is clear before any swing

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Turn side-on the opposite way to your forehand, with your hitting-side shoulder pointing toward the ball.** *Why:* Backhand uses a mirror-image turn to forehand — getting the turn right matters even more here because it's less natural.
2. **Use one hand (with a backhand grip) or two hands together if that feels more stable, and swing low-to-high.** *Why:* Two hands can give extra stability and confidence while one-handed technique and balance develop.
3. **Keep your swing path low-to-high, brushing up the back/side of the ball, the same as forehand.** *Why:* The same lifting path applies to both sides — it's the grip and turn that change, not the overall shape of the swing.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Bounce & Balance Relay (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Backhand Self-Drop Target" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Same as a forehand self-drop target game, but on the backhand side — self-drop and backhand toward a large marked zone.
- **Coaching point:** Turn your hitting shoulder to the ball first.
- **Easier variant:** Bigger target zone, closer distance.
- **Harder variant:** Smaller target zone, further distance.
- **Chaos lever:** Scoring streaks

**Skill Game 2 — "Feeder & Hitter — Backhand" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** One partner gently feeds to the backhand side, the other returns it toward a target; swap roles regularly.
- **Coaching point:** Two hands together if one hand feels shaky.
- **Easier variant:** Feeder stands close with a slow, gentle feed.
- **Harder variant:** Feeder varies feed distance/height slightly.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation

**Skill Game 3 — "Forehand or Backhand?" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** The feeder places the feed slightly to one side or the other; the hitter must read it and choose the correct grip/turn before swinging.
- **Coaching point:** Decide your side as early as possible.
- **Easier variant:** Feeder calls the side out loud.
- **Harder variant:** No call — read it from the feed alone.
- **Chaos lever:** Random constraint calls / decision-making

**Big Game — "Backhand Target Battle" (12 min)**
- Small groups take turns self-dropping and hitting backhands at a shared set of scoring zones worth different points, rewarding a growing, controlled backhand turn.

**Warm-Down — Walk & Reach (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What felt different about the backhand turn compared to forehand?
- Who used two hands well today, or one hand confidently — what worked for them?
- What helped your backhand feel less awkward as the lesson went on?
- *(Values link — Aroha):* How did Aroha show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Turns side-on correctly for backhand
- Uses one or two hands with a stable grip
- Swings low-to-high with contact out in front

**Inclusion Notes:** Fully encourage a two-handed backhand as an equally valid technique, not a fallback; allow extra self-drop reps before adding a partner feed for less confident students.

## Lesson 7 of 20 — "Switch Hitter"

**Block 2 — Combining Strokes, Footwork & Rallying** (Lessons 6–10)

**Focus Skill:** Combining forehand + backhand in a rally

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to decide quickly between forehand and backhand depending on where the ball arrives, and to combine both inside a short rally.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... decide quickly whether to use forehand or backhand based on where the ball is
- I can... keep a short rally going by combining both strokes

**NZC Links:** Strand B · Thinking, Managing self

**School Values Focus:** Manaaki — Looking after a partner's success as much as your own — feeding, rallying, and encouraging in a way that helps them succeed too.

**Equipment:** racquets, balls, low nets/rope-and-cone ‘nets’, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing between rallying pairs; low nets/ropes checked to be secure and not a trip hazard

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Read early which side the ball is coming to (forehand or backhand side of your body).** *Why:* The earlier you decide, the more time you have to turn and set up properly.
2. **Turn side-on on the correct side as soon as you've decided.** *Why:* Committing to the turn early is what makes the actual stroke technique possible under time pressure.
3. **Reset to a ready position — racquet up, light feet — between every shot.** *Why:* A rally is a series of separate decisions; resetting means you're ready for whichever side the next ball comes to.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Partner Feed & Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Left-Right Feed Choice" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A partner feeds alternately or randomly to the forehand or backhand side; the hitter must choose and use the correct stroke each time.
- **Coaching point:** Decide early, turn early.
- **Easier variant:** Predictable alternating pattern.
- **Harder variant:** Random order.
- **Chaos lever:** Random constraint calls

**Skill Game 2 — "Cooperative Rally Count" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Partners rally back and forth over a low net/rope, counting consecutive controlled hits that combine both sides.
- **Coaching point:** Aim to a spot your partner can actually reach.
- **Easier variant:** Bigger target area, closer together.
- **Harder variant:** Further apart, deliberately alternating sides.
- **Chaos lever:** Scoring streaks

**Skill Game 3 — "Mirror Ready Position" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** No ball — a partner calls or points forehand or backhand, and the student must snap into the correct ready turn as fast as possible.
- **Coaching point:** Turn your shoulders, not just your feet.
- **Easier variant:** Slower, predictable calls.
- **Harder variant:** Fast, random calls.
- **Chaos lever:** Beat the clock

**Big Game — "Combo Rally Challenge" (12 min)**
- Small groups/pairs try to build the longest cooperative rally combining forehand and backhand over a mini net, with the class tracking its best consecutive-hit total.

**Warm-Down — Partner Stretch (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What helped you decide which side to hit from quickly?
- Who kept a great rally going with their partner — what made it work?
- What did resetting your ready position do for your next shot?
- *(Values link — Manaaki):* How did Manaaki show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Reads which side the ball is arriving on early
- Turns to the correct side without hesitation
- Resets to a ready position between shots

**Inclusion Notes:** Allow a bounce or two before requiring a stroke for less confident students; adjust net height and distance so rallies are genuinely achievable for every pair.

## Lesson 8 of 20 — "Quick Feet"

**Success Criteria (WILF):**

- I can... move quickly and stay balanced to reach the ball, not just stretch with my arm

## Lesson 17 of 20 — "Dynamic Duos"

**Block 4 — Match Play & Culmination** (Lessons 16–20)

**Focus Skill:** Small-sided mini-tennis games (doubles) — communication & positioning

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to play doubles mini-tennis, communicating with a partner and covering court space together.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... call ‘mine’ or ‘yours’ clearly and communicate with my partner during play
- I can... position myself to cover space without colliding with my partner

**NZC Links:** Strand B, C · Relating to others

**School Values Focus:** Aroha — Being patient and kind with yourself while a new skill feels awkward, and encouraging classmates whose attempts look different from yours.

**Equipment:** racquets, balls, low nets/marked mini-courts, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Clear, loud calling to prevent collisions between doubles partners; standard spacing between simultaneous matches

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Call ‘mine’ or ‘yours’ clearly and as early as possible when a ball comes toward the middle.** *Why:* An early, clear call prevents collisions and confusion far better than reacting silently.
2. **Position yourself relative to your partner — side-by-side or one-up-one-back — to cover the court as a pair.** *Why:* Doubles is a shared space; a good position depends on where your partner is, not just where the ball is.
3. **Move and communicate together as the point develops, adjusting your shared coverage.** *Why:* A doubles team that moves as a unit covers far more court than two players moving independently.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Team Shape Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Call It!" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Pairs practise calling ‘mine’ or ‘yours’ loudly and early as balls are fed toward the middle of their side.
- **Coaching point:** Call it the instant you decide, not after you've moved.
- **Easier variant:** Balls fed clearly to one side or the other.
- **Harder variant:** Balls fed down the middle, genuinely ambiguous.
- **Chaos lever:** Random constraint calls

**Skill Game 2 — "Court Coverage Shuffle" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Pairs practise a simple movement pattern (e.g. one up, one back, or side-by-side) shuffling to cover the court as a feeder varies shot direction.
- **Coaching point:** Move together, not just individually.
- **Easier variant:** Slower, predictable feeds.
- **Harder variant:** Quicker, varied feeds.
- **Chaos lever:** Shrinking/growing space

**Skill Game 3 — "Doubles Feed Points" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Short doubles points started by a feed, focusing on communication and positioning rather than winning outright.
- **Coaching point:** A clear call beats a fast reaction with no call.
- **Easier variant:** Extra time between feeds.
- **Harder variant:** Quick succession of feeds.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation

**Big Game — "Doubles Mini-Tennis Round Robin" (12 min)**
- Pairs rotate through short doubles matches on modified courts, applying communication and court coverage inside real doubles points.

**Warm-Down — Circle Stretch & Cheer (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did calling ‘mine’ or ‘yours’ do for you and your partner today?
- Who covered the court really well as a doubles pair — what did they do?
- What's one thing that's different about doubles compared to singles?
- *(Values link — Aroha):* How did Aroha show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Calls clearly and early during doubles points
- Positions to cover space rather than crowding a partner
- Communicates and encourages a partner during play

**Inclusion Notes:** Pair students thoughtfully so communication styles work well together; simplify positioning patterns (e.g. just ‘left side/right side’) for pairs still building confidence.

## Lesson 18 of 20 — "Game, Set, Smart"

**Block 4 — Match Play & Culmination** (Lessons 16–20)

**Focus Skill:** Scoring & game strategy basics

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to understand and use simple tennis scoring, and to apply one basic strategic idea inside a game.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... keep score using simple modified scoring
- I can... explain one simple strategy idea and try to use it in a game

**NZC Links:** Strand B, C · Thinking

**School Values Focus:** Mōhio — Understanding your own body and how it moves — noticing exactly what's happening in your grip, swing, or footwork, and learning from it.

**Equipment:** racquets, balls, low nets/marked mini-courts, cones, simple strategy cards

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing between matches; clear, agreed scoring routines to avoid disputes

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Learn and use a simple, agreed scoring system, calling the score clearly after each point.** *Why:* Clear scoring is what turns a casual hit-around into a real game with structure.
2. **Choose one simple strategy idea before a game or set of points — for example, aiming to a particular side.** *Why:* A single, clear strategic idea is easier to actually use in the moment than trying to think about everything at once.
3. **Notice whether your strategy is working and adjust it if it isn't.** *Why:* Strategy isn't a fixed plan — reading how it's going and adapting is the real skill being learned here.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Team Shape Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Scoring Practice" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Play short points and practise keeping score aloud using a simple modified system (e.g. first to 4, win by 2).
- **Coaching point:** Call the score clearly after every point.
- **Easier variant:** Simple point-count scoring.
- **Harder variant:** Standard modified tennis scoring.
- **Chaos lever:** Bonus objectives

**Skill Game 2 — "Strategy Card Points" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Before a short game, pairs draw a simple strategy card (e.g. ‘aim to your opponent's backhand,’ ‘bring them to the net’) and try to apply it.
- **Coaching point:** Try the strategy even if it doesn't work every time.
- **Easier variant:** One simple, obvious strategy.
- **Harder variant:** A more specific or situational strategy.
- **Chaos lever:** Random constraint calls

**Skill Game 3 — "Consistency Wins" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Points only end on a clear unforced error or a genuine winner — encouraging students to keep the ball in play rather than swinging wildly for winners.
- **Coaching point:** A rally you win by outlasting your opponent still counts.
- **Easier variant:** Bigger court, more margin for error.
- **Harder variant:** Smaller court, tighter margins.
- **Chaos lever:** Scoring streaks

**Big Game — "Scored Mini-Matches" (12 min)**
- Real scored matches using today's simple scoring system, with each pair encouraged to name and try one strategy idea during the match.

**Warm-Down — Circle Stretch & Cheer (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What did you find tricky or straightforward about keeping score?
- Who tried a strategy today — did it work, and how did they adjust?
- Why might a simple strategy be more useful than just trying to hit the ball hard?
- *(Values link — Mōhio):* How did Mōhio show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Keeps and calls score accurately and clearly
- Can name a simple strategy they tried
- Adjusts their play slightly based on how a strategy is or isn't working

**Inclusion Notes:** Offer a very simple scoring option (just a running point count) for students who find traditional tennis scoring confusing; keep strategy ideas concrete and simple (‘aim there’ rather than abstract concepts).

## Lesson 19 of 20 — "Match Day"

**Block 4 — Match Play & Culmination** (Lessons 16–20)

**Focus Skill:** Full modified games with scoring

**Learning Intention (WALT):** We are learning to play a complete modified match, applying the full range of skills learned across the unit.

**Success Criteria (WILF):**
- I can... play a full modified match using appropriate strokes, movement, and placement
- I can... score and self-officiate fairly during a match

**NZC Links:** Strand B, C · Managing self, Relating to others

**School Values Focus:** Manaaki — Looking after a partner's success as much as your own — feeding, rallying, and encouraging in a way that helps them succeed too.

**Equipment:** racquets, balls, low nets/marked mini-courts, cones

**Safety Considerations:** Standard spacing between simultaneous matches; agreed, fair self-officiating routines to prevent disputes

### Skill Breakdown

1. **Bring together serving, rallying, movement, and placement into one connected match, rather than treating them as separate skills.** *Why:* A real match asks for all of these skills together, often in quick succession — this is the whole unit coming together.
2. **Call the score and any line decisions honestly and clearly, even when a call goes against you.** *Why:* Self-officiated tennis relies entirely on honesty — fair play is as much a part of the game as any stroke.
3. **Stay calm and reset after a lost point, rather than carrying frustration into the next one.** *Why:* A short memory for mistakes is what lets you play your best tennis point after point.

### Lesson Body (45 minutes)

**Warm-Up — Team Shape Jog (5 min):** Themed to today's focus skill.

**Skill Game 1 — "Warm-Up Rally Points" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** A short rally-based warm-up where students settle into rhythm and remind themselves of key technique points before matches begin.
- **Coaching point:** Use these points to find your rhythm, not to win.
- **Easier variant:** Longer warm-up time.
- **Harder variant:** Shorter warm-up, straight into matches.
- **Chaos lever:** n/a

**Skill Game 2 — "Skill Focus Choice" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Each student picks one skill from the unit (e.g. serve placement, net finishing) to focus on today, and gets brief feedback on it during play.
- **Coaching point:** Pick something you want to genuinely improve, not just something easy.
- **Easier variant:** Teacher/peer suggests a focus.
- **Harder variant:** Student justifies their own choice of focus.
- **Chaos lever:** Role rotation

**Skill Game 3 — "Practice Match Sets" (6 min)**
- **Setup/How to play:** Short practice sets (a small number of games) played with full scoring, as a run-through before the tournament.
- **Coaching point:** Treat this like the real thing.
- **Easier variant:** Shorter sets, generous court/net.
- **Harder variant:** Standard sets, standard court/net.
- **Chaos lever:** Head-to-head mirror

**Big Game — "Full Modified Matches" (12 min)**
- Students play complete modified matches (singles or doubles) with real scoring, self-officiated, drawing on serving, rallying, footwork, net play, and decision-making from across the whole unit.

**Warm-Down — Circle Stretch & Cheer (5 min):** Light movement and static stretch.

**Discussion & Reflection (5 min):**
- What part of your game are you most confident in going into tomorrow's tournament?
- Who showed great self-officiating and fair play today?
- What's one thing you'd still like to work on?
- *(Values link — Manaaki):* How did Manaaki show up in today's session?

### Teacher Support

**Look-Fors:**
- Plays a complete match applying a range of skills from across the unit
- Self-officiates honestly and fairly
- Shows resilience after lost points, staying in the match mentally

**Inclusion Notes:** Match students thoughtfully so games are competitive and enjoyable for both players; offer flexible court/net/scoring adjustments so every match remains genuinely playable.
